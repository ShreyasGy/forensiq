STATEMENT — VERBATIM TRANSCRIPTION
================================================================================

I want to make clear upfront that I am completely cooperative. I have nothing
to hide here. I run that trail almost every morning — it is part of my routine
for about two years now.

This morning I left my apartment at around 6:15, maybe 6:20. I'm pretty
consistent — I almost always leave at 6:15. I drove to the park, which is
about a five-minute drive. I parked on Birchwood Lane like I always do, right
by the green recycling collection point near the gate.

I started running at about 6:30. I had my earphones in, listening to a true
crime podcast — I listen to it every morning. I do the east loop first, which
goes past the duck pond, past the maintenance shed, then comes around north
through the wooded trail. That is where I found her.

It was about 6:42 when I saw her. She was face down in the water. I stopped
immediately. I could see she was not breathing. I did not touch her or go near
her — I stayed on the path. I called 911 right there and then and stayed on
the line until the police arrived.

I did not see anyone else on the trail at all. No other runners, no dog
walkers, nobody. It was completely quiet that early on a weekday.

Here is something I think is important — I actually ran that exact same trail
the evening before, Thursday evening. I sometimes go in the evening too if I
miss my morning session. I would say it was around 7 PM when I was on that
north trail. I can say with certainty the body was NOT there at 7 PM Thursday.
The path was completely clear.

I noticed she was not wearing any shoes. I don't know exactly why that
stood out to me but it did — you notice things when something is wrong.

Oh — I should mention. When I was walking back to my car after calling 911,
I noticed a vehicle parked on Birchwood Lane that I hadn't really registered
when I arrived. A dark-coloured SUV. It was parked facing the wrong direction
for that side of the road — like someone had pulled up in a hurry or come
from the opposite direction. It was there when I arrived, I think, but I
didn't pay attention to it at the time. Dark blue or black, I couldn't say
for certain. I wasn't close enough to read the plate.

I think that covers everything. I didn't touch anything. I didn't go near
the water. I just called 911 and waited.

Actually, wait — I said I had my earphones in. I should correct that. I had
my left earbud in but my right earbud has been broken for about a week. The
right one just stopped working. So I was effectively only listening in one
ear. By the time I got to the wooded trail section I'd actually taken the
left one out too, because I like to listen to the environment in that part
of the route — birds, whatever. So I had no audio in at all when I found her.
I would definitely have heard anyone or anything nearby.

with earphones in. He later revised this to state his right earbud was broken
and he removed the working earbud before reaching the key location. This
revision improves his claimed perceptual ability but was not offered
voluntarily — it came after a pause.

registered to Thomas Ryle, 88 Clearwater Drive — the same residential street
as the witness. Relationship between Marcus Webb and Thomas Ryle is unknown.
